# Round 3 blind critique (fresh critic, folder path only, 2026-10-03 12:05)

**First impression (2 sentences):** A golden-hour prairie with a classy, serif "nature documentary" interface: it reads as a premium indie sim, and the dusk tallgrass and snowy dawn shots would make me stop scrolling. Then I'd notice that the bison are a brown carpet of overlapping beans, the wolves are a few grey flecks I have to hunt for, and the "kill" shot is a smudge in the dirt.

**Scores (1 to 10):** Visual quality 6 / Readability 4 / Sense of life and motion 5 / Ecosystem believability (from the charts) 4 / UI clarity 7 / "Would I keep watching?" 5

**Top issues, ranked by how much fixing each would help:**

1. **Critical: the graphs show jitter around a flat line, not an ecosystem.** In the 20-seed chart, bison and pronghorn swing between about 300 and 2,500 every few years in every seed, and the medians sit flat (bison about 1,200, pronghorn about 600) for 670 years. Wolves sit at 40 to 80 at the bottom of every panel and never visibly respond to prey booms. There's no lag, no wolf boom after a prey boom, and no wolf crash after a prey crash. Bison going from 797 to 1,273 between late summer and early winter of year 1, and quadrupling within a few years in the charts, isn't possible for an animal that has one calf a year. Why it hurts: "booming, crashing, recovering" is the product, and right now the predator is decoration while the prey jitter like insects. What I'd change: slow ungulate breeding to realistic rates (roughly 20 to 30% a year at most) and make predation a real top-down force, so wolf numbers rise and fall a few years behind prey in multi-decade cycles you can actually read.

2. **Critical: you can't see the predation.** At the default wide framing (01, 06, 09) the wolves are a handful of pale grey specks at the edges. The ravens-on-a-kill shot (03) is a dark lump with a trampled fan and no identifiable birds. The top map (09) turns herds into ant swarms and loses the wolves completely. Why it hurts: the hunt is the drama, and a viewer who can't find it sees grazing wallpaper. What I'd change: a director camera that cuts to chases and kills, a subtle highlight or rim on predators at wide zoom, and carcasses that read clearly (a darker body, ravens circling and then landing in a visible group).

3. **Major: bison herds render as solid blobs and geometric rings.** In 01 and 08 the bison are packed shoulder to shoulder and overlapping, a single mass with no individual silhouettes. In 06 two herds form near-perfect donuts, in 07 a smooth crescent, and in 09 more rings and arcs. Why it hurts: it looks like a flocking bug, not grazing. Real grazing bison spread out loosely with gaps and stragglers, and travel in strung-out lines. What I'd change: more personal space, looser grazing spread, follow-the-leader lines when moving, and break up any circular milling.

4. **Major: pronghorn die out in 5 of 20 seeds with no shocks at all** (seeds 4, 5, 12, 13, 18, the last one almost immediately). In seeds 4, 12 and 13 the bison expand straight into the gap. Why it hurts: a 25% extinction rate in the "no disasters" baseline means a quarter of players lose a species while doing nothing. It also looks like pronghorn are losing a head-to-head fight for the same grass, when real pronghorn mostly eat forbs and shrubs and barely overlap with bison. What I'd change: give pronghorn their own food niche (forbs and sage patches) so they coexist with bison instead of competing on the same resource.

5. **Major: evolution is over by year 100, and it contradicts the visuals.** Bison speed jumps to about 1.47, sight drops to about 0.62 and herding falls to about 0.12, and all three pin there with almost no spread across all 20 seeds for the remaining 570 years. Why it hurts: every world converges to the same fast, near-blind, solitary bison. There's nothing left to watch after the first century, and the big herds you advertise in year 1 should dissolve. The arms race does show (wolf speed climbs to track bison), but it ends at a wall. What I'd change: add trade-offs (fast and large should cost energy, and herding should protect against wolves) so traits keep shifting instead of hitting limits. Ignore the last 30 to 40 years of the overlay charts: the medians spike there as seeds drop out.

6. **Major: the palette is one amber filter.** In 01 to 04 and 10, grass, terrain, rocks and animals all sit in the same ochre band with low value contrast, so at wide zoom species differ only by size. Only the blue-grey wolves pop, and only up close. Pronghorn read as foxes (fox-orange body, white underside). Dusk (06) is a murky purple-brown wash where nothing reads. What I'd change: pull grass toward varied straw, green and sage, darken the bison to chocolate, give pronghorn tan with bold white bands, and lift contrast at dusk.

7. **Major: the trees and rocks break the illusion up close.** In 05 the leaves are flat square cards floating off the branches like torn confetti, with bare limbs poking through. In 08 they still carry brown card-clumps in early winter. Rocks are identical grey "pillow piles" (02) sprinkled evenly across the plain (10), where they're easy to confuse with grey or white-rumped animals. What I'd change: denser, softer canopy clusters, more varied rock shapes and colors, and clustered rather than uniform placement.

8. **Minor: rendering artifacts.** A white jagged sawtooth shape at lower right in 10. In 09 the top map is soft and hazy, with a hard straight world edge at the right and dark blue circles that read as holes. Grass smears into streaky flat texture at distance (04, right side). The grass glitters like static at night (07). Bison on snow sit on dark stain patches (08). "Midwinter, snow" in 11 looks like sand dunes, while "early winter" in 08 looks properly snowy.

9. **Minor: ravens bounce off an invisible ceiling.** All 20 seeds show the same metronome sawtooth, peaking at exactly about 320 every year for 670 years. Why it hurts: anyone glancing at the graph reads "scripted." What I'd change: tie raven numbers to carcass supply, so they boom after hard winters and big wolf years.

10. **Minor: UI occlusion and graph legibility.** The god-tool column permanently covers the left edge (pronghorn and a tree hidden behind it in 03 and 05), and the Chronicle grows to two-thirds of the screen height (11). The expanded graph (10) uses a log scale that squashes four species into flat lines, with an unlabeled grass axis bolted on the right. The territory labels are hard to read: "Willow pack" is pink on tan, and "Switchgrass pack" sits under the time bar. The speed readouts disagree: 1x shows "1 year every 4 min" but 100x shows "1 year every 18 s", which is really about 13x. The trait readouts look like draggable sliders. There's no visible hide-UI or ambient mode for the second-monitor use case.

**What's working:**
1. The lighting and the grass. The low dusk tallgrass shot (05), the snowy dawn with long tree shadows (08) and the cloud shadows rolling over the plain (01) are genuinely leave-it-running beautiful.
2. The documentary voice. Chronicle lines like "Panic takes the herd, and 190 bison thunder across the plain," the "A pack falls silent" title card, and the wolf card's "One of the first wolves in the valley" and "Nothing to tell yet. Give it time." give the sim a personality most sims never get.
3. The seasons are real: late summer to bare-tree snow within year 1, with clearly different moods for midday, dusk, night and dawn.
4. Persistence: bison, wolves and ravens survive in 20 of 20 seeds over 671 years, nothing collapses or freezes into a flat line, and wolf speed coevolving with bison speed is a real arms-race signal.
5. Named wolf packs with territory rings, dens, and packs forming and breaking up add a social layer that gives the predator side stories to follow.

**The single change that would help most:** Make the wolves actually regulate the grazers, with realistic slow ungulate breeding and predation strong enough to matter, so the graphs show lagged boom, crash and recover cycles instead of jitter, and the hunts that drive them happen often enough to catch on screen.
