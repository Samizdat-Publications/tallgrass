# Round 6 blind critique (stills 01 to 11, charts 12 to 15)

Fresh blind-critic subagent, given only this folder path. Build b679901 (deployed), captured 2026-10-04 about 01:40.

**First impression:** The golden-hour aerials of a tallgrass valley with cottonwoods along the creek, the serif chronicle and the "Stampede on the plain" title cards look like a premium nature-documentary toy I would wishlist on sight. Then the wide shots and the charts show the problems: the animals turn into dark specks, and the population lines jitter like static instead of telling a story.

**Scores (1 to 10):** Visual quality 7 / Readability 5 / Sense of life and motion 7 / Ecosystem believability 4 / UI clarity 7 / Would I keep watching 6

## Top issues

1. **Critical: the population dynamics are fast noise, not booms and busts, and shocks heal almost at once.** Every seed settles by about year 100 into a stationary band. In the 60-year close-up, seed 1 bison go from 145% to 27% and back to 150% of their mean in about 5 years; seed 5 pronghorn swing from 300% to 10% within 3 years. A 70% bison cull is back to baseline in about 5 years; with wolves wiped, bison jump from about 1,000 to over 3,000 almost at once. In-game, bison rise 830 to 1,018 and pronghorn 374 to 559 between late summer and early winter of year 1, while real bison and pronghorn calve only in spring. Pronghorn repeatedly hit near zero (seeds 1, 4, 7, 17, 18) and go extinct in 2 of 20 baseline worlds. Fix: real, seasonal birth and maturation rates, capped yearly growth, cycles of 10 to 30 years, and recoveries from a cull that take a decade or more.
2. **Critical: wolves and ravens are disconnected from the food web.** Ravens sit on a flat ceiling (about 320 in every scenario panel, about 280 in all 20 baseline seeds), and the in-game raven sparkline climbs in stair steps. Wolves hover at 50 to 60 whatever happens; a 70% bison cull barely dents them. In the 60-year close-up, wolves peak in the same year as bison instead of lagging. Fix: tie wolf births and pack survival to kill rate for the classic lag; let ravens follow carcass supply.
3. **Major: evolution is over by year 150, and it goes the wrong way.** Bison speed (about 1.47) and size (about 1.45) shoot up and then flatline with almost no spread, as if at a ceiling. Bison herding collapses from 1.0 to about 0.13; sight drops to about 0.63. Pronghorn speed barely moves (1.0 to 1.1). Wolf traits drift randomly. Fix: give speed and size real costs, make herding pay off against wolves, avoid hard ceilings.
4. **Major: wolves vanish at any zoom except a close follow.** No wolf could be confidently found in shots 01 and 04 to 09. In 02 the inspector says "Chasing prey" with no prey in frame, three wolves merge into one blob, and the selection mark is a yellow smudge rather than a crisp ring. Fix: a subtle rim light or marker on predators at distance; frame hunter and target together; dust or motion trails in chases.
5. **Major: the season readout contradicts the world.** In 11 the header says "early summer, afternoon, clear", the banner says "Year 3, mid-spring", and the ground and trees are under deep snow.
6. **Major: wide and map views turn the herds into stains.** In 06 the bison read as black burn scars or mold. In the 09 top map the animals look like hair clippings or iron filings, and species can't be told apart. In 01 the main herd is a dark carpet. Fix: species-tinted, slightly brightened stand-ins at distance, lifted dusk ambient, species colors in the map view.
7. **Major: distant terrain and the world edge fall apart.** In 04 the top half of the frame is a smeared, low-res brown watercolor; the 09 map edges show horizontal streaks; in 06 beyond the valley is a flat hazy plain with evenly spaced identical lollipop trees; in 07 there are radial streaks at the top of the night frame. Fix: a distant grass and color layer, a designed world edge (ridge, fog or vignette), less uniform trees.
8. **Minor: herds behave like formations, and the animals clip into each other.** Bison bodies overlap into a solid mass (02, 04). Herds form neat rings and crescents with empty centers (01, 02, 09). Fix: more spacing, aligned headings while grazing, a looser spread.
9. **Minor: close-up and low-angle art is weaker than the aerials.** In 05 the trees have thick black trunks and sparse pom-pom foliage and look charred, and the golden dusk shot has an empty grey sky with no sun glow. In 04 the rocks are flat-shaded and faceted. In 03 and 04 the green bushes are blotchy flat sprites. In 08 the snow patches read as blue puddles. The wolf at the bottom left of 03 looks like crumpled paper.
10. **Minor: UI rough edges.** The chronicle order is inconsistent: in Year 2 "falls apart" is listed above "is founded", while Year 1 reads oldest first. Older chronicle entries drop off without a scroll cue. The "Sickness in the herd" banner covers the Cottonwood pack label. The UI colors don't match the animals. "100x asked, running at 23x" shows that centuries take hours. Panels cover about a third of the screen, with no clean ambient mode.

## What's working

1. The golden-hour aerials (01 to 03) are genuinely beautiful: tallgrass texture, long shadows, creek, ponds and worn game trails.
2. The seasons are real and dramatic, and bison spread out and walk single file in the snow.
3. The narrative layer: the serif chronicle, named packs, event title cards, and the territory overlay with dens.
4. The individual inspector (02) is excellent: founder-versus-now trait ticks, life story, follow.
5. The baseline holds together for 700 years with no bison or wolf extinctions across 20 seeds. Ravens circling a carcass (03) is a lovely moment.

**Single change that would help most:** realistic, seasonal birth and maturation rates, with wolves and ravens depending on their food with a lag, so the graphs show readable booms and busts over decades and every shock leaves a scar that takes years to heal.
