# Round 9 blind critique (10:19 Sunday, stills of the 09:51 build aee1c66, captured from a clean worktree with ?dim=0)

Note: the four chart images in this folder were copied from round 8 (made at 09:02) and predate the 09:22 chart fixes (game colours, shock lines and prior bands drawn for the merged suite, legend only lists what is drawn). The chart complaints in issue 10 refer to those stale copies.

**First impression (2 sentences):** The golden-hour opening shot of a tallgrass valley with a pond, autumn cottonwoods and nearly a thousand bison, plus a narrated chronicle, looks like a premium nature sim, and I would click on it. Then the winter, drought and wide shots turn into purple-brown mud with dark smudges for herds, and I'd start to suspect that one pretty angle is carrying the store page.

**Scores (1 to 10):** Visual quality 7 / Readability 5 / Sense of life and motion 6 / Ecosystem believability 5 / UI clarity 7 / "Would I keep watching?" 6

**Top issues, ranked by how much fixing each would help:**

1. **Critical. Predators and scavengers don't react to their food.** What I see: in the 20-world chart, wolves sit in a flat 45 to 65 band in every seed while bison swing from 500 to 2,000. Ravens are a ruler-straight line pinned at about 300 in every world and every scenario, which reads as a hard ceiling. In the drought scenario, bison and pronghorn crash to near zero around year 250, and wolves only dip from about 52 to about 38 before bouncing straight back. Ravens crash to zero during that die-off, which is backwards: a mass die-off is a carrion feast, so ravens should boom first and crash after. In the 60-year closeup, the wolf line stays within about 20% of average and shows no lag behind the prey peaks. Why it hurts: the brief is "predators hunt and multiply" with booms and crashes, and the predator line is the one viewers watch for drama. Right now it's flat. Fix: tie wolf births and survival tightly to kill rate with a one-to-two-year lag, so a prey crash causes real wolf starvation. Drive ravens from carcass supply and remove the cap, so they spike after die-offs and hard winters.

2. **Critical. Evolution saturates by year 300 and then moves the wrong way.** What I see: bison herding drops from 1.0 to about 0.13 within about 250 years in every seed. Bison sight drops to 0.63. Bison and pronghorn litter size both climb to the same ceiling of about 2.38. Bison speed caps at about 1.45. After year 300 every bison and pronghorn trait is a flat line, and the wolf traits just wander. Why it hurts: "thousands of generations" has nothing to show after the first few hundred years. Bison giving up herding under wolf pressure is the opposite of real bison, and it erases the game's best visual, the huge herd. A bison litter of 2.4 is also unrealistic, since bison have single calves. Fix: make herding pay off under predation (shared vigilance, safety in numbers) and give traits costs that shift with predator density, so wolves and prey push each other back and forth in an ongoing arms race. Cap bison at one calf.

3. **Major. Cold-season, drought and wide-shot art falls apart.** What I see: in 06 (evening wide), the terrain is mottled purple and blue like crumpled foil, herds read as brown mold, trees are orange matchsticks, the river is a flat black ribbon, and the pond is a black hole with a purple rim. In 11, drought ground is mauve rather than bleached straw. In 07, the night pond has a hard white ring around it. In 05 (named "golden_low" but showing early-winter dusk), the frame is mostly blue-grey grass. Why it hurts: about half the season and weather cycle looks muddy, and a second-monitor watcher will spend a lot of time in exactly these states. Fix: grade winter toward desaturated white-blue with warm sun accents, and keep straw grass showing through snow. Make drought pale, bleached yellow with cracked bare ground. Give water a sky reflection and a soft shoreline, and drop the black core and the hard white ring.

4. **Major. Herds render as one overlapping carpet, and animals clip into each other.** What I see: in 01 and 07, hundreds of bison overlap into a continuous dark mass. The hilltop herd in 02 is a single brown lump. In 02, the wolf pack is three or four grey bodies fused into one blob, and the selection ring sits beside the wolf rather than under it. At wide zoom (06, 09), dark bison on dark ground read as dirt stains. Why it hurts: you can't count, follow or care about individuals, and the herd looks like texture rather than animals. Fix: enforce spacing of one to two body lengths, give herds loose real structure (files, sub-groups, a leading edge), and add a lighter back or rim light so bison separate from shadow at distance.

5. **Major. Animals form donut rings around the water.** What I see: in the top-down map (09), the animals sit in near-perfect circles around each pond, while the lower half of the map is almost empty. Why it hurts: from above it reads instantly as an AI artifact, not a landscape being used. Fix: have animals come to water along trails, drink at a few access points, then disperse to graze, and spread grazing pressure across the whole map.

6. **Major. The UI boxes in the action and hides the subjects.** What I see: the tool column, population panel, chronicle and speed bar frame all four sides and cover about a third of the screen. In 03, the wolves that made the kill sit underneath the population panel. In 02, a wolf runs under the tool column. In 05, the herd is hidden behind the tool column. The large centered event titles ("Stampede on the plain", "Sickness in the herd") sit right over the middle of the scene. Why it hurts: this is supposed to be ambient viewing, but the frame reads like a dashboard with a landscape behind it. Fix: add an idle or ambient mode that fades all panels after a few seconds. Move event titles to a lower-third card that fades, and have the camera keep the subject clear of panels.

7. **Major. Events are told, not shown.** What I see: "44 fall ill" and "Sickness in the herd" appear, but no animal on screen looks sick. The "Stampede" frame doesn't clearly show running animals: no dust, no motion streaks, no direction. The chronicle adds nothing from early winter of year 1 through late spring of year 2, even though ravens dropped, wolves jumped from 30 to 39 and bison fell from 955 to 908 on screen. Why it hurts: the chronicle is the best storytelling tool here, and if the screen doesn't back it up, the words feel invented. Fix: give events visible tells (sick animals lagging with heads low and duller coats, dust plumes trailing stampedes, snow drifts and carcasses for winterkill). Cut the camera to the event when its title fires, and log winter entries such as first snow and winterkill counts.

8. **Major. The opening is fragile and slow to settle.** What I see: in the scenario chart, median bison fall about 70% in the first decade and need 150 to 200 years to level out. Seed 11 loses all four species inside about 100 years, and several thin raven lines die out in the first 50 years. At 1x, a year takes 2 minutes, so an hour of viewing covers 30 years. At 100x, the HUD admits the machine is running at 34x. Why it hurts: a typical viewer watches the start-up period, not the ecosystem, and 1 in 20 watches everything die. Fix: start worlds near their long-run balance by running the sim ahead before showing it, add a rescue floor for the first decades, and make fast-forward actually hit its advertised speed.

9. **Minor. Some props look like placeholders.** What I see: rocks are flat tan ovals that look like potatoes, with no texture and no grass around the base (04 bottom right, 08 center). In 05, the bare winter trees repeat the same spiky silhouette, so the valley reads as a burnt dead forest rather than dormant cottonwoods. Why it hurts: these break the painterly look in exactly the close shots that need to hold up. Fix: use faceted rock meshes with grass at the base, vary tree silhouettes and branch density, and keep a few evergreens or leftover leaves.

10. **Minor. Several readability nits in UI and effects.** What I see: rain is drawn as screen-space diagonal streaks even in the straight-down map view (09), so it looks like scratched glass. In the expanded graph (10), axis labels from different rows stack into each other (bison "800" directly above wolf "60"). The animal panel shows raw decimals such as "coat 0.28" without saying what they mean. In the territory overlay, the pink Switchgrass pack is nearly invisible against the mauve ground. In the scenario chart, the legend promises dashed red shock markers and blue prior-range bands that I can't find, and the subtitle ends in a stray word, "commit". Fix: draw rain in world space or fade it out in top-down view, add spacing between graph rows, add plain-language trait captions, use more saturated territory tints with outlined edges, and clean up the chart legend.

**What's working:**
- The golden-hour lighting in 01 to 04 (low sun, long tree shadows, glowing tallgrass, yellow cottonwoods ringing the pond) is good enough for a store-page hero shot.
- The Chronicle's writing voice is excellent. Lines like "Panic takes the herd, and 86 bison thunder across the southwestern plain," stamped with the season, make the sim feel authored.
- The individual-animal panel is the right documentary hook: pack membership, founder status, life story, a follow button, and trait bars with ticks for where the founders started and where the species is now.
- Herbivore dynamics in the long runs are believable. Bison and pronghorn swing irregularly between about 50% and 230% of their averages, pronghorn are more volatile than bison (which is right for the species), and 19 of 20 baseline worlds run past 1,000 years.
- The wolf territory overlay, with named packs, pack counts and den marks, plus the expanded small-multiples graph with grass cover, gives the god-view player real information.

**The single change that would help most:** Tie wolf and raven numbers directly to their food (kills and carcasses), with no hard caps and a visible lag, so the predator lines boom and crash after the herds instead of sitting flat.
